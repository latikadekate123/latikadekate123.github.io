    Research: why simulated traffic lights fail on real streets

    Signal controllers trained with reinforcement learning look great in a simulator, then stumble at real intersections. Sim2Signal pulls that sim-to-real gap apart into four pieces and tests 18 mitigation methods across them.

    Sim2Signal: Sim-to-Real Benchmarks for Traffic Signal Control

    F. Al Rafi, S. Mukherjee, L. L. Dekate, et al. Nine authors, Arizona State University.

    arXiv:2609.01676, September 2026. Preprint, under review at AAAI 2027.

    Done during my research internship at ASU's DaRL Lab with Prof. Hua Wei, May to August 2026.

    Observation

    The controller sees noisier or different data than in training.

    Action

    Signal changes take effect late. Tested with delays of 20 to 60 seconds.

    My focus: I ran and validated these experiments. Try it in the live toy.
    Transition

    Real traffic responds differently than the simulator predicts.

    Reward

    What the controller optimizes drifts from what matters on the road.

    Across the whole project: 18 mitigation methods, 2 RL controllers, 33 gap settings and 10 calibrated networks from 5 locations. Of the four gaps, only the action gap had fixes that held up across the networks tested.
